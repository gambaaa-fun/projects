The History of The Company

The entity known merely as "The Company" operates as a monopolistic, faceless megacorporation dominating the scrap recovery industry. They specialize in sending contracted employees to abandoned, heavily industrialized exoplanets to recover lost technology, raw materials, and bizarre artifacts.

Their methods are ruthlessly efficient and sociopathic. Employees are forced into strict, unforgiving quota systems. Failure to meet these quotas results in immediate and lethal termination—often by being jettisoned into the vacuum of space. The Company provides no hazard pay, no medical benefits, and actively charges its employees for travel expenses to the more lucrative, dangerous moons.

SIGURD'S LOG // SYSTEM ENTRY 001 DATE: AUG 22, 1968
> It's been a week since we started working for the Company.
> The quotas are getting absurd. We barely survived the last trip to March.
> I keep hearing strange noises coming from the wall of the Company building when we sell our scrap. It sounds like something massive is breathing behind the desk.
> Rich says I'm just paranoid from the lack of sleep, but the building shakes when it exhales.

There is a growing theory among veteran scavengers that "The Company" is not a corporation in the traditional sense. The massive, tentacled entity residing behind the counter at the 71-Gordion facility consumes the scrap rather than processing it for sale. It is highly probable that employees are not gathering scrap for profit, but are unwittingly feeding an ancient, cosmic horror.

The Abandoned Facilities & Experiments

The concrete facilities and grand mansions scattered across the moons were clearly built by humans, likely centuries ago during an era of rampant galactic expansion. However, they have been abandoned for an unknown amount of time, left to rot alongside their secrets.

"These places weren't built for us to scavenge. They were built for people to live and work. Now look at them. Just steel tombs waiting for the next crew to make a mistake."

The architecture suggests a mix of deep-core mining, hazardous material containment, and advanced biological research. The presence of entities like the Coil-Head (a mechanical weapon designed to freeze upon observation) and the Old Birds (massive, autonomous war machines) indicates these planets were either active warzones or weapons testing facilities.

Many of the biological horrors, such as the Thumper and the Bracken, show signs of aggressive genetic mutation. It is heavily implied that the original inhabitants of these facilities were either wiped out by their own creations or evacuated in a panic, leaving the machines and mutants to evolve in the dark.

The Sigurd Tapes

Scattered throughout the solar system are hidden data logs left by a previous Company employee named Sigurd, dating back to 1968. He was a member of a crew consisting of himself, Rich, Desmond, and Jess. His logs provide a grim look into the psychological toll the job takes and serve as a warning to all future crews.

SIGURD'S LOG // SYSTEM ENTRY 009 DATE: SEP 03, 1968
> We lost Desmond today.
> One of those spring-headed things got him in the dark. He blinked. That's all it took. I heard the snap from two corridors away.
> We couldn't even recover his body to avoid the penalty discount. The ship autopilot engaged at midnight and left him behind.
> The Company sent an automated condolence message to the terminal. It misspelled his name as 'Desi'. They don't care. We are just meat for the machine.

As Sigurd's logs progress, his mental state deteriorates. He becomes obsessed with the golden voices he hears on the terminal and the true nature of the beast at the Company building. The ultimate fate of Sigurd and his crew remains a dark mystery, though it is almost certain they did not survive their contract.

The Moons & The Void

The moons themselves are anomalies. They orbit dead or dying stars in a sector of space that seems disconnected from the rest of human civilization. The weather patterns on these moons are violently erratic, shifting from clear skies to eclipses and devastating floods in a matter of hours.

The further out a crew travels—towards moons like Rend, Dine, and Titan—the more the fabric of reality seems to fray. These moons cost a significant amount of credits just to route the autopilot, leading to speculation that the Company actively discourages travel to these sectors, or perhaps the toll is paid to whatever forces govern those frozen wastes.
