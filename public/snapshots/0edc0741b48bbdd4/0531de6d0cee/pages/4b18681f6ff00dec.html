Who we are

MORE THAN A CLUB

Glowride started as a small group of skaters in Jablonec nad Nisou who just wanted a place to roll, learn, and belong. Over a decade later we're still here — bigger, faster, and louder. Whether you're a complete beginner clicking into skates for the first time or a seasoned rider hunting new lines, you'll find your people here.

Skating styles

Beginner friendly

Freestyle Slalom

Weave through cones with precise, flowing footwork. One of the most technical disciplines on blades — and one of the most addictive. Perfect for beginners who want to build real control fast.

All levels

Freeskate

Roll through the city, explore trails, and skate for the pure joy of it. Freeskating is the most versatile style — relaxed distance rides, technical city lines, or fast open-road sessions. If you want freedom on wheels, this is where you start.

Beginner — Intermediate

Inline Dance

Flow to the rhythm with elegant footwork and smooth, musical movement. Dance skating combines choreography with technical precision — every trick lands to the beat. Expressive, social, and endlessly creative.

All levels

Inline Hockey

Take the rink to the street. Inline hockey is fast, physical, and tactical — quick passes, sharp stops, and full-speed sprints. A great way to skate competitively and build real teamwork at the same time.

Kids — Beginner

Freestyle for Kids

A fun, safe introduction to freestyle skating for younger riders. Sessions are built around games, simple tricks, and building coordination in a supportive group. The best way for kids to fall in love with skating.

FAQ

For freestyle skating, skip standard fitness skates and choose versatile "freeskate," "urban," or "freeride" models. Top competitive brands include Seba and Powerslide, while Rollerblade and Tempish offer great high-quality alternatives.

Rockering (often called the "banana" setup) is a wheel configuration where only the middle two wheels touch the ground, achieved by using a special frame or 4mm smaller outer wheels. While it slightly reduces forward stability, it massively improves maneuverability and rotation, making it an absolute necessity for freestyle slalom skating.

Freestyle slalom offers countless tricks of varying difficulties that skaters can combine, modify, or completely invent. Skaters progress by learning foundational moves that build into more advanced, competitive maneuvers (rated from E to A), with some high-level tricks requiring serious physical fitness.

Invest in official freestyle slalom cones, as makeshift options like plastic cups easily blow away in the wind. To set up, find a flat, safe surface and space your cones 80cm apart, which is ideal for beginners before trying the 50cm or 120cm spacings used in competitions.

For a safe and stylish night ride, consider upgrading your skates with Luminous LED wheels by SEBA. These high-visibility wheels ensure you won't be overlooked in the dark and are available in an 85A hardness across multiple colors and sizes (72, 76, and 80 mm) to fit your setup perfectly.
